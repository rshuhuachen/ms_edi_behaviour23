11  Testing for career stage sensitivity

Author

Rebecca S. Chen

How robust are our analyses to the categorisation of career stage?

Career stage is complex: the meaning of position titles can be country dependent, and career stage is not always indicative of academic age. Here, we explore how sensitive our analyses are to our definition of career stage.

11.1 Relation with age

In the manuscript, we categorise post-docs as mid-career and associate professors as senior, though post-docs could be considered early-career (depending on the time since getting their PhD) and associate professiors are less senior than full professors. First, we redefine early-mid-late career researchers using an alternative categorisation:

survey <- survey %>% mutate(career_alt = as.factor(case_when(
  career == "BSc/MSc Student" | career == "PhD student" | career == "Post doctorate" ~ "Early career",
  career == "Associate professor" | career == "Lecturer" | career == "Researcher" ~ "Mid career",
  career == "Professor" ~ "Late career",

11.2.1 Analysis of motivations and hesitations that predict question asking

Next, we fitted 19 more binomial GLMs to test which one of the motivations and hesitations was a statistically significant predictor for the probability of a person asking a question during the congress. The dependent variable in these models was therefore the response to the question “Did you ask one or more questions during Q&A sessions?” (1 for yes, 0 for no) and the response variable the binomial response whether the motivation or hesitation was ticked (1) or not (0), while again controlling for career stage.

  subset <- survey %>% filter(!is.na(.[[i]]) & !is.na(colnames(survey)[i]) &
